Where the curves come from

Body curves use the lab’s measurements. Bat curves are calculated from motion capture, one swing at a time, then averaged. The skeleton and arrows show average positions. An angle drawn on that skeleton can differ from the average of the measured angles. Averaging also smooths timing differences between hitters.

What a curve can tell you

Use the curves to compare positions and the timing of movement. Force and energy transfer require additional measurements. Sensor scores summarize parts of a swing; a continuous curve shows how a measurement changes over time.

Teaching sequence adapted from Hacking the Kinetic Chain: Hitting Biomechanics. Measurement context draws on the Driveline Blast Motion metric definitions. The catalog identifies each signal’s lab channel or reconstruction method. The strike zone and swing plane are visual references. Surface color runs from the selected signal’s lowest value (blue), through the midpoint (green), to highest (red); gray marks missing samples. These colors show values, not performance grades. The green ground trace follows center of mass. Center of pressure is a separate measurement.

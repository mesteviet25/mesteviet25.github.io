Most swing analysis is geometry. Angles, positions, paths, the shape a body makes. Useful, but it
describes the outcome of a process without describing the process.

The energy framing asks different questions. How much mechanical energy does each segment generate?
How much of it transfers up the chain instead of dissipating? Where does the handoff happen, and is
a hitter losing bat speed because they cannot make energy or because they cannot pass it along?

Those have different answers in the weight room.

## Why it appeals to me

Because "make more energy" and "leak less energy" look identical in a bat speed number and are
completely different training problems. A hitter who generates plenty and transfers badly does not
need to get stronger. Telling them to is worse than useless — it costs them a training block and
confirms their suspicion that the numbers do not describe them.

It also reframes the kinetic chain from a sequence of positions into a sequence of transfers, which
matches how it actually feels to swing. The pelvis does not "get to a position." It does work on the
thing above it.

## The honest state of it

The synthesis phase is done: reading, framing, working out which quantities are computable from what
gets captured and which ones require assumptions I am not comfortable making.

The measurement phase is where it sits now, and it is gated on a genuinely dull problem — getting at
the underlying quantities in a form that supports the calculation, rather than the derived summaries
that are convenient to hand out. Energy flow needs mass properties and full kinematics together, and
plenty of pipelines throw one of those away early because nothing downstream had asked for it.

The most reliable way to make a hitting session feel good is to make it easy to succeed. Same pitch,
same speed, same location, twenty in a row. The hitter gets into a groove, the numbers on the
readout climb, everyone leaves happy.

Then Friday comes and none of it shows up.

## Acquisition is not retention

This is one of the older findings in motor learning and one of the least absorbed. Practice
conditions that produce the fastest improvement *during* the session frequently produce the worst
retention and the worst transfer. Conditions that feel worse — messier, more mixed, more failure —
produce the opposite.

The uncomfortable implication is that in-session performance is close to useless as a signal of
whether the session worked. The thing you can watch is the thing that does not predict. The thing
that predicts happens next week, when you are not looking.

## Why blocked practice survives anyway

Because everything about it is rewarding to everyone present.

The hitter feels competent, which is genuinely valuable and not to be dismissed. The coach sees
improvement inside the block and reads it as effectiveness. The numbers go up. And nobody attributes
Friday's outcome to Tuesday's practice structure, because a week is far too long a feedback loop for
anyone's intuition to bridge.

It is a local optimum with a very comfortable floor.

## What I actually do about it

Not "make everything random." Difficulty has to be tuned to the person and the moment — too far past
the edge of capability and you are not training, you are just failing, and there is no evidence that
failure alone teaches anything.

What I aim for is: keep the reps, add the constraint. Same volume, same swings, but the hitter has to
solve something to get them. A target that has to be earned. A count that changes the objective. A
score that makes the easy solution not worth taking. The physical work is identical; the cognitive
work is not.

That is where the constrained competitive games came from. Not because games are fun — although they
are, and that matters more than the literature usually admits — but because a game supplies the
pressure and the variability for free, in a way that a coach standing there saying "now do it under
pressure" cannot.

## The honest caveat

I can tell you the practice structure changed. Proving it caused Friday requires the intervention
study I have not run, on a sample I do not have, over a season nobody wants to randomise.

So this is a bet on a well-supported general principle, not a measured local result — and I would
rather say that plainly than dress up a preference as evidence.

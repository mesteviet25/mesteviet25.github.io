Take a claim that gets made constantly, in some form, in every hitting facility on earth:

> Hitters who rotate the pelvis earlier produce more bat speed.

There are at least three completely different things that sentence can mean, and they are not
interchangeable.

**Between hitters.** Line up a hundred hitters. The ones whose pelvis fires earlier tend to swing
harder. This is a statement about how a population varies. It is heavily contaminated by everything
that travels with earlier rotation — size, strength, training age, whether they can already swing
hard and so can afford to.

**Within an assessment.** Take one hitter on one day and twenty of their swings. The ones with
earlier pelvis rotation tend to be their harder swings. This is a statement about how one person
fluctuates. It might be about effort. It might be about which pitches they chose to go after.

**Under intervention.** Take this hitter and make their pelvis fire earlier. Bat speed goes up. This
is the only one that is actually a coaching claim, and it is the only one you cannot get from
observation alone.

## Why it matters more than it sounds

These three can have different magnitudes. They can have *different signs*. There is nothing
paradoxical about a trait that correlates positively across a population and negatively within a
person — it happens whenever the population differences are driven by something the individual
change does not touch.

So when a study reports a between-hitter correlation and a coach turns it into a cue, a step got
skipped. Not a small one. The entire causal step.

## What I do about it

Say which one, out loud, every time. It is a small discipline and it costs nothing:

- "Across hitters, this goes with that" — population.
- "Within his own swings, when he does this, that follows" — fluctuation.
- "When we changed it, this happened" — intervention, and the only one that earns a training block.

The third sentence requires actually having changed something and measured it afterward. That is
inconvenient, which is exactly why the first two get quietly promoted into it.

The fastest way to find this error in your own work is to write down which question you are
answering before you run anything. If you cannot pick one, you are about to answer whichever the
data makes easiest.
